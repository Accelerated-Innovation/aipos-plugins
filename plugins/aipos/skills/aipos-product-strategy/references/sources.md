# Sources and AIPOS design choices

Primary sources reviewed on 2026-09-24. The brief is an AIPOS synthesis, not a
reproduction of either author's template or a claim they endorse this workflow.

- Roman Pichler, [Product Vision Board](https://www.romanpichler.com/tools/product-vision-board/):
  links vision to target group, needs, product characteristics, and business goals;
  its extended form adds business-model considerations. This informs the brief's
  product-level focus and distinction between purpose and strategy.
- Roman Pichler, [Product Strategy Framework](https://www.romanpichler.com/blog/romans-product-strategy-framework-v3/)
  (May 11, 2026): connects vision, strategy, outcome-based roadmaps, and backlog in
  both directions. New-product strategy is tested against major assumptions;
  results can lead to strategy changes. His starting guidance supports beginning
  with a single outcome when a longer roadmap would be speculative.
- Marty Cagan, [Assessing Product Opportunities](https://www.svpg.com/assessing-product-opportunities/)
  (December 13, 2006): advocates a lightweight assessment of the problem, market,
  alternatives, differentiation, timing, route to market, success, constraints,
  and recommendation. This informs opportunity and business viability questions.
- Marty Cagan, [Build to Learn FAQ](https://www.svpg.com/build-to-learn-faq/)
  (April 27, 2026): separates strategic selection of a problem from a team's work
  discovering a solution that achieves the outcome. Prototypes test risks; a PRD
  supplements discovery. Production results feed further learning. This informs
  our delegation to solution exploration and experiments, and return from results.
- Marty Cagan, [The Four Big Risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/)
  (December 4, 2017): distinguishes value, usability, feasibility, and business
  viability, with collaborative contributions from product, design, and engineering.
  This informs risk coverage and whom to involve, not a mandatory four-test sequence.

The authors differ in emphasis and ownership: Pichler recommends an extended
product team owning vision through backlog; Cagan describes product leaders
selecting strategic problems and empowered teams discovering solutions. AIPOS
therefore asks who owns the decision in the actual organization rather than
asserting one universal authority model. An idea-only starting point may need
problem research; an established strategic problem need not be rediscovered
before exploring solutions.

**AIPOS choices:** one canonical Product Opportunity Brief of approximately one
or two pages; graph-optional intake; claim-level provenance; explicitly proposed
versus adopted choices; four separate decision states; named skill handoffs; and
revision links to downstream artifacts. These are our integration conventions.
The brief's title, fields, storage path, and decision states are not prescribed
by either author. Their public articles are context, not evidence that a user's
specific product opportunity has been validated.
