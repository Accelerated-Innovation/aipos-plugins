# Product Opportunity Brief

Use this as a concise structure, not a questionnaire that must be completed
before delivering anything. Adapt commercial language to internal products.
Keep the main brief approximately one or two pages, with short sections and
links to supporting material. Do not fabricate content to fill a section.

```markdown
# Product Opportunity Brief — <working product name>

Brief: <canonical path or supplied record reference> · Revision: <revision/date>
Content status: <draft hypothesis | agreed hypothesis | revision proposed>
Evidence base: <sources actually read; what remains unsupported>
Strategy owner / contributors: <supplied roles or names; unknown where absent>

## Vision
<Purpose and positive change. An aspiration, not a claim that benefits occurred.>

## Initial target segment
<Primary users; buyer or internal sponsor; context and shared need.>
Choice: <proposed / adopted, with attribution if adopted>
Outside initial focus: <explicit exclusions or unresolved choice>

## Customer need
<Job, difficulty, consequence, current behavior and alternatives, with claim-level
provenance. Separate observed symptoms from any inferred cause.>

## Strategic product concept
<Proposed value, why it could stand out, why people might switch or change their
workflow. Keep the concept at product level; reference supplied fixed constraints.>

## Opportunity and strategic fit
<Why now; why this organization; relevant business objective; size only if
supported, with population, period, units, and source limits. Unknown otherwise.>

## Business viability
<Commercial: buyer, revenue/pricing hypothesis, acquisition/adoption route,
material costs and operating constraints. Internal: sponsor, benefits, adoption
and change effort, operating owner, costs and constraints. Distinguish proposals.>

## Success
Customer outcome: <desired change>
Business outcome: <desired value>
Initial measurable outcome: <one when sufficiently understood; otherwise unknown>
Measure / population / period: <known or proposed>
Baseline or comparison: <source-backed value, unknown, or reason a historical
baseline is inapplicable to an absolute test; never assume zero>
Target or decision criterion: <adopted / proposed / unknown>
Observation method and relevant guardrail: <known or proposed>

## Assumptions and risks
<The few uncertainties that could invalidate the strategy, with [A] or [I],
source references where relevant, and links to further detail.>
First uncertainty to address: <which, why, and what would change our decision>

## Next decision and learning plan
Recommendation: <action and decision sought; not authority>
Evidence needed / method: <specific observation and suitable experiment>
Owner / budget / horizon: <agreed values and source, otherwise unknown or proposed>
Status: <strategy agreement, learning authorization, investment evidence, and
production approval reported separately where relevant>
Next owner: <owning skill, brief section/revision, evidence and assumption refs>

## Revision note
<For updates: what changed, why, evidence, adoption status, downstream review needed.>
```

For an existing brief, preserve its identifier and useful structure. The template
does not authorize replacing a user's document or adding a duplicate. A review-only
request returns findings keyed to the existing sections.

If evidence needs an appendix, link a compact register with local claim/assumption
labels, actual source IDs, dates or unknown dates, population, finding, provenance,
limitations, and status. Locally assigned labels must be identified as local; do
not invent external study, customer, problem, or decision IDs. Link to existing
registers rather than reproducing them.
